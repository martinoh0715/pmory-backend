# PM Interview Preparation for Emory Students

Practical prep for Product Management interviews (APM / new-grad / internship), adapted for Emory students.
Primary external source distilled for this knowledge base: Keiana Snell’s open guide
https://github.com/keianasnell/pm-interview-prep (plus standard PM interview frameworks).

## Mindset before you grind

- Failure in FAANG/top-tech loops is common and not a measure of your worth — many strong PMs take multiple cycles.
- Protect sleep, meals, exercise, and non-tech friendships; overwork culture is not a strategy.
- Use Reddit/Blind sparingly — those communities skew extreme and can wreck confidence.
- Track applications in a spreadsheet (company, stage, referral, deadlines, next action).

## Before you apply

1. Make sure you actually want PM: read what PMs do day-to-day vs. SWE, consulting, marketing.
2. Essential books often cited by candidates: *Cracking the PM Interview* and (for light technical fluency) *Cracking the Coding Interview* — Gayle Laakmann McDowell.
3. Curated reading lists: ProductHired open product management repo; Tech Interview Handbook for process logistics.
4. Classic essays: a16z “Good Product Manager / Bad Product Manager” and modern follow-ups.
5. Internship/APM lists to browse (then narrow): apmlist.com, Product Alliance internship list, collegeproductmanager.com.

## Getting the interview

- Referrals matter a lot. Build them early via clubs, company events, alumni, and real relationships — not last-minute mass DMs.
- Prefer a shorter target list you can customize over spraying every company on a mega-list.
- Emory angle: leverage Goizueta alumni, consulting clubs, product/tech orgs, case competitions, and professors for intros.

## Interview loop overview

Typical stages (varies by company):

1. Recruiter screen / resume walkthrough
2. Phone or virtual screen (product sense + light behavioral)
3. Onsite / loop: product design, execution/metrics, behavioral, sometimes analytical or light technical/system design

## Behavioral interviews

Use **STAR**: Situation, Task, Action, Result — with quantified outcomes when possible.

What companies often probe:

- Ownership / follow-through
- Leadership and influence without authority
- Conflict and hard interpersonal situations
- Learning from failure
- Clear communication (especially about ambiguous problems)

Common prompts:

- Tell me about yourself / walk me through your resume
- Describe a challenging project
- Leadership style / dealing with failure
- Conflict on a team — how you resolved it
- Why this company / why PM / why you
- Hardest person you worked with — what would they say about you today?

Emory tip: convert class projects (MKT 442 practicum, ISOM builds, OAM entrepreneurship) into crisp STAR stories with users, decisions, and results.

## Product sense (“soft” product questions)

Always prepare a **favorite product** (app, physical, or company product) and be ready to discuss:

- What user need it solves
- Why people love it vs. alternatives
- How you’d improve it (as a PM, with insight — not random feature ideas)
- An unsuccessful product and why it failed
- Design critique using principles: usefulness, understandability, efficiency, UX delight, communication/feedback

Launch / success metrics checklist (**AARM**):

- Acquisition
- Activation
- Retention
- Monetization

## Product design (“hard” questions)

Use a structured method such as **CIRCLES** (Impact Interview / Lewis C. Lin):

1. Comprehend the situation (clarify goal, constraints, success)
2. Identify the customer / users and segments
3. Report customer needs / use cases
4. Cut through prioritization (who/what first — MVP)
5. List solutions
6. Evaluate tradeoffs
7. Summarize recommendation and metrics

Practice prompts (from common banks / walkthroughs):

- Design a microwave / kitchen / vending machine for a blind user
- Design Google Maps for schools
- Redesign a supermarket for college students
- Design a smartwatch, alarm app, or 3-button TV remote
- Improve a company product you’re interviewing for

In answers: start with the user, prioritize ruthlessly, walk a use-case end-to-end, then poke holes (offline mode, safety, cost, feedback loops).

## Execution / prioritization

Example style: “Double group joins in 3 months with 5 engineers.”

Show:

- Goal clarity and metric definition
- Cohort / segment thinking
- Prioritization and tradeoffs
- Timeboxing and sequencing
- Internal + external stakeholders affected
- How you’d measure and iterate if it fails

ROI-style tradeoff talk: compare options with estimated impact vs. cost/effort when asked to choose features.

## Analytical / estimation

- Market sizing with clear assumptions stated out loud
- Metric diagnosis / root cause (brainstorm causes, then systematically rule out — fishbone diagrams help)
- Comfort talking about funnels, conversion, retention curves

## Technical fluency (PM-level, not LeetCode-max)

Many PM loops are light on whiteboard coding; still know fundamentals.

- Be able to discuss how you’d partner with engineers
- Light data structures / complexity intuition can appear in screens
- System design awareness: APIs, latency, load, storage at a conceptual level (especially Google/Microsoft-style loops)

Resources often used: Interview Cake, LeetCode/HackerRank for refreshers; system-design primers for high-level architecture.

## Sample phone-screen flow (pattern)

1. Resume deep-dive on something you’re proud of — why it fits this company
2. Favorite product at that company
3. New feature idea for a team — tie to mission
4. How you’d measure success
5. If it fails, what’s the next idea?
6. Short leadership / challenge story

## Emory-specific preparation tips

- Name real coursework when relevant: **MKT 347** (product/brand), **BUS 360** (design thinking), **ISOM 352** / **MKT 342** (analytics), **MKT 443** (pricing/monetization).
- Use Goizueta case interview habits for structured thinking — then add user empathy and metrics.
- Bring campus evidence: shipped class products, consulting practicum clients, entrepreneurship builds, user interviews you ran.
